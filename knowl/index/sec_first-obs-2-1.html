and noticed that the sun appeared to move around the Earth. Based on this, a Greek astronomer, Claudius Ptolemy proposed the Geocentric Theory in 150 AD, which said that the Earth is fixed at the center of the universe and the sun revolves around it. This theory was accepted for about 2,500 years since it could predict the positions of celestial objects fairly accurately. However, it failed to properly explain the backward movement of planets, known as Retrograde Motion . Retrograde Motion

Planets like Mars appear to move backward in the sky for a short period before resuming their normal direction. This is called Retrograde Motion. Ptolemy’s Geocentric Theory could not explain this properly. Hence, in 1543, Polish astronomer Nicholas Copernicus introduced the Heliocentric Theory, which stated that the Sun is fixed at the center and all planets, including Earth, orbit around it. This theory successfully solved most astronomical puzzles of that time. Copernicus also proposed that Earth has three motions spinning daily on its axis, orbiting the Sun every year, and a slow wobble of its axis over time.
